## 2. Learning objectives

Explain the questions addressed by dendroecology. Relate growth changes, scars and ages to forest history. Distinguish observed changes from hypotheses about causes. Consider not only how growth changes but also which comparisons can help evaluate its possible causes.

## 3. Can trees preserve evidence of insect feeding?

## 4. Three kinds of evidence for forest history

Growth: changes in ring width and their duration. Injuries: dated scars and subsequent wound closure. Populations: regeneration, establishment and mortality. Growth, injuries and population ages provide different information. Combining them helps evaluate the limitations of any single record.

## 5. Canopy gaps and changing competition

A canopy gap changes light and competitive conditions. Responses depend on species, position and prior growth. Reconstruct stand dynamics from multiple individual histories. Responses to extra light differ among species and prior growth states. Consider the surrounding conditions before and after a gap opens.

## 6. Growth suppression and release

Suppression: a period of relatively slow growth. Release: a sustained increase in growth to investigate. One wide ring alone does not establish canopy opening. A sustained increase can be informative, while a single wide ring does not by itself establish release from competition.

## 7. Different causes can produce similar changes

## 13. Release criteria depend on the trees

## 14. Ring width and basal area increment

BAI measures the increase in stem cross-sectional area. For a circular stem: BAI = pi [(r + w) squared − r squared]. Equal widths can add different areas; BAI alone is not biomass. Basal area increment is a geometric stem measure. Converting it directly to carbon would ignore volume and wood-density information.

## 15. Inferring establishment from tree ages

## 16. The bias in looking only at survivors
