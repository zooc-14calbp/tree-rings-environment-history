# Lesson 5: Dendroarchaeology — handout
Reading Environment and History through Tree Rings | 90 minutes | Draft v1 | 30 October 2026

## Central question
What can a calendar date assigned to wood tell us about human activity? Distinguish the outer-ring date, felling date and construction date. Explain conclusions and limits using preservation and archaeological context.

## Interpretation sequence
1. Identify species, measurement path and preservation; crossdate the ring series.
2. Establish whether the last preserved ring is the original outermost ring.
3. Record bark edge, sapwood and the heartwood–sapwood boundary.
4. Separate final growth, felling, working, storage, construction and use.
5. Interpret dates with member positions, joints, tool marks, repairs and deposition.

## Qualifications
Even a complete bark edge may belong to a tree that died before it was cut. A completed outer ring can indicate the following dormant interval, which may span calendar years. Seasonal interpretations require anatomical observations and local growth phenology.

A terminus post quem (TPQ) excludes earlier dates; it is not a precise construction date. Unknown loss of outer rings leaves the upper bound unresolved. Sapwood estimates need an appropriate species and regional reference population: there is no universal number to add.

## Minimum record
Include specimen ID, structural location and relationships, species, measurement path, last ring, bark and sapwood preservation, crossdating evidence, meaning of the date, assumptions and alternatives. Record undated members too. Multiple members can come from one tree and may not provide independent confirmation.

## Separate levels of evidence
Write a short statement distinguishing observations, conditional inferences and unresolved questions. A cluster of dates does not exclude storage or same-tree origin. If the original-member assumption fails, revise the main-frame inference.

## Compare published reports
Mitsutani (2001, pp.32–33) reports a 594 CE ring date for the Horyuji central pillar, discussing outer-wood preservation and three additional outer rings. Does a single member date establish the date of the entire building? See R31 for the original images and graph.

The Rook Hall Farm report (R32) derives winter 1522/23 felling from complete sapwood in one sample and suggests construction in 1523 or within a year or two. This is a contextual interpretation, not a universal storage allowance.

# Terms and further study
| Term | Brief explanation |
| --- | --- |
| dendroarchaeology | Using tree rings to study archaeological material and history. |
| last preserved ring | Youngest surviving ring; not necessarily the original outermost ring. |
| bark edge | Original outer wood surface directly beneath bark. |
| sapwood | Outer wood outside the heartwood; ring count varies. |
| heartwood–sapwood boundary | Boundary used as the origin for total-sapwood estimates. |
| terminus post quem (TPQ) | Earliest bound: an event cannot be earlier than this. |
| felling date | Time of cutting, distinguished from ring formation. |
| building phase | A proposed stage of construction, extension or repair. |
| reused timber | Timber used again after an earlier use. |
| dendroprovenancing | Investigating timber origin using ring patterns and other evidence. |
