| Term | Meaning in this session |
| --- | --- |
| Sample | Material selected for study, such as wood, a seed or a mineral. Check its relationship to the event of interest. |
| Relative age | The order of events: which came first? |

# Samples and the events we can date

Use this table during the exercise. You do not need to memorise the method names in advance.

| Method or approach | Example material | Dated event and checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relative dating | Stratigraphic layers | Establishes order. Check overturning, redeposition and mixing. |
| Dendrochronology | Wood with tree rings | Dates surviving rings. Assess missing outer wood and reuse before interpreting felling or construction. |
| Radiocarbon | Carbon in charred seeds or wood | Relates to carbon incorporation. Check contamination and context, then calibrate to calendar time. |
| K-Ar / Ar-Ar | Suitable volcanic minerals | Relates to cooling and gas retention. Check minerals and depositional context. |
| U-Th | Suitable carbonates, including stalagmites | Dates carbonate formation under suitable conditions. Assess initial material and later exchange. |
| Luminescence | Sediment or heated material with suitable minerals | Assesses time after signal resetting. In the laboratory, OSL uses light and TL uses heat. |
| Archaeomagnetism | Fired material or kilns | Records cooling after sufficient heating. Check the regional reference curve and later movement. |

Relative versus numerical concerns age expression. Direct versus indirect concerns the relationship between a sample and the target event. Growth, radioactive decay, stored signals and magnetism describe different clocks. These perspectives can apply to the same measurement.

## Environmental archives

Tree rings, lake varves and ice layers form through different processes. A varve is a set of sediment layers representing one year. Visible bands require checks for annual formation, continuity and dating. Ring width can also respond to age, competition or insect damage as well as climate.

| --- | --- | --- |
| B: timber | Dendrochronology | Dates surviving rings. Missing outer wood prevents an immediate felling or construction date. Check for reuse. |

## Reference years and notation

1000 ± 30 BP is an example of a conventional radiocarbon age referenced to 1950. Subtracting it from the current year is incorrect. Subtraction from 1950 does not produce a calendar age either.

Calibrated ages may use cal BP, cal BCE or cal CE. The reference year for cal BP is also 1950. Record the calibration curve, assumptions and the way uncertainty is reported. Historical CE/BCE notation has no year zero.

A calendar-dated ring does not by itself explain the cause of an event. Establish the date before interpreting environmental change or human activity.

# Lesson 1  Connecting tree rings and radiocarbon

For slide 11 and Lesson 3, slide 13. A four-minute check; no preparation is required for Lesson 1.

## Two clocks, connected by known-age wood

Crossdate rings to establish their calendar years. Measure their radiocarbon content. Many pairs of known calendar ages and measured radiocarbon ages provide evidence for a calibration curve. Tree rings are a major source of calibration data, but do not supply the entire curve.

For an unknown sample, use the relationship in the other direction: compare the radiocarbon measurement and its uncertainty with an appropriate curve to obtain calendar-age ranges and probabilities. Atmospheric radiocarbon has varied, so subtracting a radiocarbon age from 1950 does not by itself give a calendar year.

| Question | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Calendar age of a ring | Crossdating against a securely dated reference |
| Calendar age of an unknown sample | Radiocarbon measurement, uncertainty and suitable curve |
| Date of an archaeological activity | Relationship between growth, use, deposition and the activity |

**Why measure radiocarbon in wood whose calendar age is already known? Explain the separate roles of crossdating and radiocarbon measurement.**

## Check

The independently known calendar age makes the ring a calibration reference. Irregularities in the curve can produce several possible calendar-age ranges for one measurement.
