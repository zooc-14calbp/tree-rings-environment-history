## 8　From stem to cambium: where is new wood added?

The vascular cambium lies between phloem and xylem. It produces secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward. New wood lies outside older wood, not at the pith. Tissue widths are schematic. Upper stem portions formed later, so ring counts also depend on sampling height.

[Source 1](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook) · [Source 2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2016.00734)

## 13　Separate cell enlargement from wall formation

Follow a simplified tracheid through development. Division produces a new cell; enlargement increases its dimensions. Secondary-wall material is deposited inward and lignification proceeds. Maturation removes the cell contents, leaving the lumen and wall. Distinguish enlargement of the outline from thickening of the wall. Phases partly overlap, and neighbouring cells may differ in stage. Living sapwood parenchyma does not share this same fate.

[Source 1](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2016.00734) · [Source 2](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook)

## 17　Tracheids and vessels: where does water pass?

Tracheids overlap, and water moves between neighbouring cells through pits in their side walls. A pit retains a pit membrane; it is not simply an open hole. Vessel elements join end to end through perforations, forming vessels; their side walls also contain pits. Compare connectivity, not dimensions. The following transverse photographs view these cells end-on.

## 24　Read a ring boundary using anatomical evidence

Both panels show the same Scots pine transverse image. First find the band of cells with smaller lumens and thicker-looking walls, then the neighbouring cells with larger lumens. The right panel labels the structures that guide interpretation. The dark band itself is not a single boundary line. Examine where latewood ends and the next earlywood begins using cell structure, then trace the candidate position laterally. Check other radii or specimens as well. This slide does not establish a ring count or a calendar date.

[Source 1](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Earlywood-latewood_PISY.jpg)

## 25　A dark band need not mark a new year

The photograph is an example inherited from earlier teaching materials. Do not equate dark-band counts with years. The diagrams explain alternatives and do not trace this photograph. One shows a candidate transition from latewood to the next earlywood; the other shows latewood-like cells followed by a return to earlywood-like cells within a growing season. Compare cells before and after a candidate band, then check other radii and crossdate.

[Source 1](https://www.ltrr.arizona.edu/skeletonplot/ringanomalies.htm)

## 26　Discontinuous rings: check more than one radius

This Scots pine image is published as an example of a discontinuous ring. The two vertical guides mark different approximately radial observation paths. They are not established ring-boundary traces. Find a visible growth layer near one guide and follow its lateral continuation. Failure to observe one ring at a particular position does not establish a whole-tree absence of growth in that year. A layer visible elsewhere may narrow and terminate. Do not draw missing tissue into the image to obtain a count. Check another radius and crossdate against other specimens.
