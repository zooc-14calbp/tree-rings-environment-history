### Slide 3　Inferring felling from the outermost ring

How closely can the outermost surviving ring constrain felling? Compare bark, sapwood, and heartwood preservation and the evidence needed for year or season estimates. By the end, you should be able to distinguish a felling estimate from a lower bound using the preservation evidence.

### Slide 4　Learning objectives

Explain the questions addressed by dendroarchaeology. Distinguish outer-ring, felling and construction dates. Use preservation and context to state conclusions and limits. The aim is to explain the evidence linking a numerical date to an event, not merely to report a year.

### Slide 5　From measurement to archaeological interpretation

Crossdating assigns calendar years to preserved rings. The last measured ring may not be the original outermost ring. Now interpret the dates in their archaeological context. Even with secure measurement and crossdating, interpreting construction or repair requires the object's structural and archaeological context.

### Slide 6　Ring years and event dates

Ring formation. Felling. Working / storage. Construction / use. Repair / reuse / deposition. Intervals can separate the stages shown. Before equating felling with use, ask what evidence constrains the time between them.

### Slide 7　Outer-edge preservation changes the date interpretation

These schematics compare different degrees of outer-edge preservation. The upper sample retains bark and may approach the final growth event. The middle sample ends within sapwood, leaving an unknown outer portion. The lower sample retains only heartwood, so the number of lost outer rings is unknown. ### Slide 8　What does a surviving bark edge establish?

Inspect the outermost increment beneath the bark. Check incomplete growth and possible cutting after tree death. Relate final growth to felling only with supporting conditions. A complete outer edge tightly constrains felling, but intra-ring position and dormancy can still matter to the event interval.

### Slide 9　Estimating missing sapwood

Identify the heartwood–sapwood boundary. Use a sapwood model appropriate to species and population. Do not transfer another region’s ring-count range uncritically. Estimating missing sapwood requires appropriate species and regional information, rather than adding a universal number of rings.

### Slide 10　Read “1580 or later” on a timeline

Suppose the last surviving ring dates to 1580. The timber must have been felled after that ring formed, so felling cannot precede 1580. The rightward arrow indicates an unresolved latest limit. Its endpoint is not a particular calendar year. With unknown outer-ring loss, we cannot simply add a fixed number of years. Further constraints require preservation evidence, a heartwood–sapwood boundary, an appropriate regional and species model, or independent building records. Do not plot this result as a point date for construction.

### Slide 11　Considering the felling season

Examine earlywood and latewood development. Growth timing varies with species, site and climate. A winter interval may span two calendar years. Felling-season inference examines the developmental state of outer cells and requires evidence about preservation and the growth season.

### Slide 12　Connecting timbers with building history

If one timber is older, does that make the whole building older? Use sampling locations, independent trees, and date groups to compare construction, repair, and reuse. By the end, you should be able to explain which interpretation fits the evidence and what would help distinguish alternatives.

### Slide 16　Three members need not represent three trees

In this fictional example, one tree provides members A and B, while another provides C. Three members therefore represent two trees. Similar series from the same tree are not independent confirmation from three trees. Evaluate same-tree candidates using distinctive sequences, anatomy, dimensions and member records as well as curve similarity. The arrows assume a known origin for explanation. In real evidence, that origin itself may be a hypothesis requiring verification.

### Slide 21　Comparing building reports

Which timbers provide the dating evidence in a building report? Read the Horyuji, Yakushi-ji East Pagoda, and English cases in terms of timbers, matches, and interpretations. By the end, you should be able to explain how far the dated timbers support conclusions about construction or repair.

### Slide 22　Timber provenance is a separate question

Dendroprovenancing compares candidate source regions. The best match need not identify a unique origin. Consider reference coverage, timber trade, species and anatomy. A regional chronology match does not by itself establish timber origin. Consider broad climate coherence and the available reference coverage.

### Slide 23　Reading a Japanese report: Horyuji

Mitsutani (2001) reports a 594 CE date for the central pillar. Three further outer rings extend the measured sequence ending in 591. What evidence connects a member date to the date of the building?. Consult the primary report for material condition, the event constrained and the wording of its conclusion, rather than filling gaps from a summary.

### Slide 24　Yakushi-ji East Pagoda: dates by timber

Read the Yakushi-ji East Pagoda table from the top. The lower central pillar retains an outermost ring dated seven nineteen. It retains sapwood and lacks bark. Two boards from the first story retain bark and give dates of seven twenty-nine and seven thirty. Preservation determines what each date can establish. Compare these results with written records and architectural evidence when discussing construction.

### Slide 25　Reading an English report: Rook Hall Farm

Complete sapwood in one sample supports winter 1522/23 felling. The report suggests construction in 1523 or within a year or two. Separate the felling result from the construction interpretation. Reports in another language can be read using the same checks: material, measurement, dating evidence and structural context.

### Slide 26　Record undated material as well

Short series, weak patterns or missing references can prevent dating. Dated members may not represent the whole structure. State what remains undated and propose further observations. Unsuccessful dating has causes worth recording. Reporting only successful samples can give a misleading picture of the available material.
